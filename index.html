The Service

Precise shower valve work, not generic plumbing

A shower valve controls both flow and temperature. When the cartridge wears or the body fails, you get drips, sudden temperature swings, or a handle that no longer works smoothly.

We focus on identifying whether a cartridge swap is enough or whether the full valve body needs replacement—then completing the work with proper access, correct parts, and a watertight finish.

Process

How shower valve replacement works

Clear steps from diagnosis to a working shower—no unnecessary wall damage when a cartridge is the real issue.

01

Diagnose the failure

We determine whether the cartridge, O-rings, or the valve body itself is causing the drip, temperature swing, or stiff handle.

02

Access the valve

Most cartridge work is done from the finished side. Full body replacement often uses an access panel on the opposite wall when available.

03

Replace with correct parts

Matching brand and model cartridges or a compatible new valve body ensure proper fit, pressure balance, and long service life.

04

Test and restore

Water is restored, the system is checked for leaks and temperature control, and the trim is reinstalled cleanly.

Why it matters

A failed valve affects more than comfort

Constant drips waste water and can damage surrounding tile or drywall over time. Temperature spikes create safety issues. A stiff or broken handle makes daily use frustrating. Addressing the valve correctly restores reliable control and protects the surrounding structure.

Questions

Common questions about shower valve work

Most dripping and temperature issues are solved by replacing the cartridge inside the existing valve body. A full body replacement is typically needed only when the body is corroded, cracked, or you are changing valve styles.
Cartridge replacement is almost always done from the finished side without cutting tile. Full valve body work preferably uses an access panel from the opposite side of the wall when one exists or can be created cleanly.
A straightforward cartridge swap is often completed in under an hour once the correct part is on site. Full valve body replacement takes longer depending on access and pipe connections.
Cartridge swaps are manageable for experienced homeowners with the right tools and the correct part. Full body replacement involving soldering, push-fit connections, or limited access is best handled by a professional to avoid water damage.
